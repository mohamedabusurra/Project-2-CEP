## 📌 Topic Summary

In February 2024 a finance worker in Hong Kong joined a routine video call with his CFO and colleagues. Every face and voice on that call was generated. He authorised 15 transfers totalling about US$25.6 million.

That case is the headline, but it is not the story. The story is that the cost of producing a convincing forgery of a real person collapsed to near zero — three seconds of audio clones a voice, a public conference talk clones a face — while our ability to detect one did not improve at all. Humans separate real from synthetic at roughly coin-flip accuracy.

The result shows up in three places at once: on your phone as a scam call in a family member's voice, in your feed as fake news carrying the borrowed credibility of a real trusted face, and in markets, where a fabricated video of an exchange CEO can move retail money before anyone can debunk it. And the most common use of the technology has never been financial at all — it is non-consensual intimate imagery of real people.

Two laws came into force in the months before we submitted this: the US TAKE IT DOWN Act removal obligations (19 May 2026) and the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules (2 August 2026).

## 🔑 Key Findings

- **US$893.35 million** in AI-related fraud losses across 22,364 complaints logged by the FBI for 2025 — the first year IC3 counted AI as its own category (published April 2026).
- **US$2.19 billion** in documented global deepfake fraud losses since January 2019, of which **US$1.65 billion was 2025 alone** (Surfshark, April 2026). Fewer than 5% of voice-clone victims are estimated to report at all, so every total is a floor.
- **1 in 5** biometric fraud attempts is now a deepfake, across more than a billion identity checks (Entrust 2026 Identity Fraud Report).
- **~50%** — human accuracy on high-quality fakes, statistically close to a coin flip; only 0.1% of participants in iProov's 2025 study could reliably tell the difference. Detection tools scoring ~96% in the lab lose 45–50% of that accuracy in the field.
- **48 hours** — the window US platforms have had since 19 May 2026 to remove non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI-generated forgeries, after a valid request (TAKE IT DOWN Act; FTC enforcement, penalties to $53,088 per violation).
- **€15M or 3% of global turnover** — maximum penalty under EU AI Act Article 50, applicable from 2 August 2026, which requires deepfakes and AI-generated public-interest content to be disclosed and machine-readably marked.
- **A methodological finding:** widely circulated "surge" statistics for this topic (900%, 1,210%, 2,137%, 3,892%) are measured over different windows on different denominators and cannot all be true. See §8 of `research-notes/research-material.md` for what we used and what we rejected.
